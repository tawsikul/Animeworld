# Anime World

A rebuild of the original site. Same idea, new design system, and the broken
parts actually work now.

## What's new in v5

- **Find by scene** (`find.html`, also the camera button in the home search bar). Three ways to identify an anime:
  - **Screenshot** — matched against trace.moe's frame database; comes back with the title, episode, timestamp and a similarity score, and opens straight into the detail sheet. Drag-and-drop, tap to choose, or paste an image.
  - **Clip** — three frames are pulled from the video on the device (the video never uploads), each is matched, and the results are merged.
  - **Sound** — type a lyric, title or artist and it's matched against AnimeThemes' opening/ending database, then resolved to the catalogue. Recording the music playing nearby also works once a free [audd.io](https://audd.io) key is set as `auddToken` in `site-config.js`; without one, the text route still works.
- Free tier of trace.moe allows a limited number of searches per month per visitor; the page says so plainly if the limit is hit.

## The watch link

The old build pointed at `anikoto.to`, which doesn't resolve — hence the DNS error. Searching turns up a dozen look-alike domains for that name (`.uno`, `.net`, `.cz`, `.su`, `.com.co`, `anikototv.to` …), several of which warn about the others being malware mirrors. There's no way to tell which one is "real", so nothing is hardcoded.

Instead, `site-config.js` decides where the button goes:

- **Left empty (default):** it opens the official streaming page for that exact title — Crunchyroll, Netflix, Hulu, HIDIVE and so on — straight from the catalogue's licensed links. That lands on the show, not a search, and it's the only kind of link that can be guaranteed to work.
- **Set `watchUrl`:** paste any site's search URL with `{q}` where the title goes, plus a display name, and the button and hand-off screen use that instead. Your site, your call.

If a title has no official stream listed, the button falls through to the guide page lookup.

## Run it

It's plain HTML/CSS/JS — no build step, no dependencies.

- **Locally:** `python3 -m http.server 8000` in this folder, then open `http://localhost:8000`
- **Hosting:** drag the whole folder into Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages

Opening `index.html` straight off the disk mostly works, but browsers block some
requests on `file://`. Use the local server if anything looks empty.

## What was broken, and what changed

**Search dropped your keystrokes.** An `isLoading` flag made the old loader
return early, so anything you typed or changed while a request was in flight was
silently thrown away. Requests are now keyed per channel — a newer one always
supersedes the older one, and nothing gets ignored.

**Results arrived out of order.** Typing fast meant an older response could land
last and overwrite newer results. Stale responses are now detected and dropped.

**Searching buried the thing you searched for.** The old query kept sorting by
popularity while searching, so exact matches sank. A search now sorts by
relevance unless you deliberately pick a sort.

**Filters had no way back.** Added active-filter chips, individual removal, a
clear-all button, and URL sync so results are shareable and the back button works.

**Season filter returned nothing.** AniList needs a season *and* a year together.
Picking a season without a year now fills in the current year automatically.

**Login did nothing at all.** With the placeholder Firebase keys, `initFirebase`
bailed out before it ever rendered the nav button, and the forms silently failed.
Accounts now work immediately, stored on the device, and upgrade to synced
Firebase accounts the moment real keys are present in `firebase-config.js` — no
other code changes.

Also added: rate-limit handling with a retry, adult titles filtered out, image
fallbacks so a poster never renders broken, and keyboard support throughout.

## The character stage

Clicking any card opens a detail sheet that does a bit of theatre:

1. The site's accent colour is taken over by that series' palette
2. Speed lines burst, the screen flashes, and a figure enters from the left
3. An aura pulses, embers drift up, and a short musical phrase plays
4. Hitting **Watch now** makes the figure point at the logo, the logo ignites, a
   beam crosses the screen, and the tab opens on Anikoto

The figure is picked by matching the title against a keyword table
(`ARCH_RULES` in `stage.js`), falling back to genre. Ten archetypes — shinobi,
blade, hero, voyager, sorcerer, pilot, scout, ace, spirit, and a default.

To add your own, drop a rule in `ARCH_RULES`, a palette in `ARCHETYPES`, a pose
in `figureSVG()`, and a phrase in `MOTIFS`.

### On the figures and the audio

Both are generated in the browser at runtime. The figures are original geometric
silhouettes drawn as SVG paths — they represent a *role*, not any specific
character, so nothing copyrighted ships with the site. The sound is synthesised
live with the Web Audio API: original five-note phrases, one per archetype, plus
impact and charge cues. No soundtrack files are used.

If you want real character art or actual theme music, you'd need to license them.
Anything else risks a takedown, and the whole site would be at stake.

Sound is **off until you turn it on** via the speaker button in the nav — browsers
block audio before a click anyway, and autoplaying at someone is rude.

## Optional: synced accounts

Accounts work out of the box on the device. To make one login work across phone
and laptop, follow the six steps at the top of `firebase-config.js`. The site
detects real keys and switches over on its own. Lock down your Firestore rules
before going live — the snippet is in that file.

## Data

Everything comes live from the [AniList](https://anilist.co) GraphQL API:
titles, artwork, scores, airing times, and the streaming links on the guide page.
Nothing is hardcoded, so nothing goes stale. Artwork belongs to its rights holders.

## Accessibility

Keyboard navigable throughout, visible focus rings, labelled inputs, and
`prefers-reduced-motion` respected — the entrance animations and particles switch
off for anyone who's asked their OS for less motion.
